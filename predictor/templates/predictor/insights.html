How the Heart Disease Predictor Works

An overview of the machine learning pipeline, clinical parameters, and dataset used to build this heart disease risk prediction application.

1. Model Overview & Specifications

This project uses a K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) classification algorithm implemented with Python's Scikit-Learn library. KNN was chosen for its intuitive approach: to assess a new patient's risk, it looks at the 5 most similar patients in the historical dataset and calculates the probability based on their known cardiac outcomes.

2. How Patient Data is Processed

Step 1 — Data Collection: The patient's vitals (age, blood pressure, cholesterol), symptoms (chest pain, exercise angina), and ECG metrics are collected through the web form.
Step 2 — One-Hot Categorical Encoding: Categories such as chest pain type, resting ECG, and ST slope are transformed into numerical binary columns, matching the format the model was trained on.
Step 3 — Feature Standardization: Because metrics are measured on vastly different scales (e.g. cholesterol is ~200 mg/dL while ST depression is ~1.0 mm), a StandardScaler centers each feature around zero with unit variance so no single feature unintentionally dominates the prediction.
Step 4 — KNN Distance & Probability: The model computes Euclidean distances to find the 5 closest patient cases and returns both the predicted class (0 = Low Risk, 1 = High Risk) and the percentage probability.

3. The 15 Features Used by the Model

These 15 parameters are read from heart_columns.pkl and provided to the model:

Feature Name Type Measurement Unit / Options Why It Matters
Age Number Years (18–95) Risk of coronary artery disease increases with age.
RestingBP Number mm Hg Chronic high blood pressure strains heart arteries.
Cholesterol Number mg/dL High cholesterol contributes to arterial plaque buildup.
FastingBS Binary 0 = ≤120 mg/dL, 1 = >120 mg/dL Indicator of diabetes or pre-diabetes.
MaxHR Number bpm (beats per minute) Maximum heart rate achieved during exercise testing.
Oldpeak Number mm (ST depression) Depression in the ST wave during stress vs at rest.
Sex_M Binary 1 = Male, 0 = Female Biological sex marker.
ChestPainType_ATA Binary 0 or 1 Atypical Angina discomfort.
ChestPainType_NAP Binary 0 or 1 Non-Anginal chest pain.
ChestPainType_TA Binary 0 or 1 Typical Angina (classic heart chest pain).
RestingECG_Normal Binary 0 or 1 Standard electrical conduction at rest.
RestingECG_ST Binary 0 or 1 Resting ST-T wave abnormalities.
ExerciseAngina_Y Binary 1 = Yes, 0 = No Chest pain triggered by physical exercise.
ST_Slope_Flat Binary 0 or 1 Flat ST wave during peak exercise.
ST_Slope_Up Binary 0 or 1 Upsloping ST wave (usually a healthy response).

4. Dataset Information

The model was trained on the renowned UCI Heart Disease Dataset (available on Kaggle), combining clinical records from 5 reputable medical institutions: the Cleveland Clinic Foundation, the Hungarian Institute of Cardiology, the University Hospital of Zurich, the University Hospital of Basel, and the V.A. Medical Center in Long Beach, California.
